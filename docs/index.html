U.S. and Global Interest Rates

Equilibrium Real Interest Rate

The inflation-adjusted interest rate consistent with the economy running at full strength over the long run — often called r*.

UC r* is from a compact Unobserved Components model estimated by economist Michael Kiley — the same model behind the Unobserved Components output gap on the Resource Utilization and Risk page. It's shown alongside four independent outside estimates — Laubach‑Williams (the NY Fed's HLW model), an SPF‑implied rate (from the Philadelphia Fed's professional-forecaster survey), an SEP‑implied rate (from the FOMC's own longer‑run projections), and the TIPS 5y5y forward real yield (a market-based estimate) — plus the realized real fed funds rate, which fluctuates around all five as actual policy oscillates above and below r* over the cycle.

UC r* based on M.T. Kiley (2020), “What Can the Data Tell Us about the Equilibrium Real Interest Rate?,” International Journal of Central Banking, 16(3), 181–209.

Laubach‑Williams from Holston, Laubach & Williams (2017, updated 2023), “Measuring the Natural Rate of Interest,” Federal Reserve Bank of New York.

UC r* — from a small Unobserved Components model of U.S. GDP growth, unemployment, inflation, long‑run inflation expectations, and the fed funds rate, re-estimated (posterior-mode) each update. One-sided (filtered, real-time) estimate shown — no uncertainty band, since none exists for the one-sided version.
Laubach‑Williams — the NY Fed's HLW natural-rate model, a semi-structural Kalman-filter estimate jointly using trend growth and the output gap. One-sided by construction. Pulled from the latest vintage sheet of the NY Fed's own published workbook (updated quarterly); earlier vintage sheets in that same file (real-time snapshots as the estimate looked historically) are not used here.
SPF‑implied — the Philadelphia Fed Survey of Professional Forecasters' median 10‑year‑ahead annualized projection for the 3‑month T‑bill rate (BILL10), less its median 10‑year‑ahead CPI inflation projection (CPI10). Annual, not quarterly — BILL10 is asked only in the Q1 survey each year, unlike CPI10 (quarterly), so this line has one point per year.
SEP‑implied — the FOMC's own median “longer run” federal funds rate projection from the quarterly Summary of Economic Projections, less the median longer‑run PCE inflation projection (both from FRED: FEDTARMDLR, PCECTPIMDLR). Updates at each SEP release (four times a year); PCE longer-run projections are only available on FRED from mid-2015 on, so this line starts later than the others.
TIPS 5y5y — the 5‑year, 5‑year‑forward real yield implied by 5‑year and 10‑year constant‑maturity TIPS yields (DFII5, DFII10, both from FRED), a market-based expectation of the average real short rate 5 to 10 years out. Unlike the other four lines, this embeds a term premium, not a pure r* expectation — it has tended to run somewhat above the model- and survey-based estimates for that reason.
Realized real fed funds rate — the effective federal funds rate less trailing 4‑quarter core PCE inflation, both from FRED. This is not an r* estimate; it's actual monetary policy, shown to fluctuate around the r* lines as the stance of policy moves above and below its estimated neutral level over the cycle.
